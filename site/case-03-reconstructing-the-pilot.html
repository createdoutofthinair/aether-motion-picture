Black Star: reconstruct the pilot

On this pageRebuild the asset chainRehearse one difficult cutReview boundaries and handoff

Rebuild the asset chain

The production can be reconstructed from the source treatment, the numbered still folder, the clip manifest, the completed result ledger, and the local video files. Preserve the relationship between these assets with one stable ID per setup. For this project, the manifest filename is the join key for the still and rendered clip; 066A and 066B remain separate rows. Keep the prompt alongside the still so a reviewer can see which visual setup and camera direction produced each render.

A repeatable sequence is:

  1. Read the treatment and continuity notes; decide the story action, frame, and one primary camera movement for each shot.
  2. Generate one still per setup from the artwork palette and approved subject references. Check 16:9 framing and save a 1920×1080 file with a stable, zero-padded name.
  3. Review the still sequence for character, costume, prop, lighting, and screen-direction continuity before generating motion.
  4. Freeze the prompt, still, duration, model, aspect ratio, resolution, and output count in a row-level manifest.
  5. Check account capability and available balance before a paid request. Submit each unique row once; capture its complete result and poll its task to a terminal state before recording the output.
  6. Download only the selected completed work, keep signed links out of logs, and validate the local file’s size, container header, dimensions, duration, and decodability.
  7. Extract extra frames from high-risk clips, then review full motion and the actual edited sequence against the audio master.

The downloadable shot list contains the 87 editorial rows with ID, filename, requested duration, story action, camera direction, and prompt. It is a reconstruction aid, not a provider transaction ledger. Lettered IDs are separate setups where the treatment or manifest split one numbered beat: examples include 056A/056B and 066A/066B. Keep the suffix when matching stills, prompts, and renders.

Rehearse one difficult cut

Before a new full-length batch, test a short sequence that crosses the hardest visual changes. A practical adjacent handoff pilot is clips 025→026. Clip 025 slides laterally until the captain’s reflection aligns with the faceless silhouette; clip 026 continues the reveal at the observation window with a shoulder-level slide. Review the cut between them for matching screen direction, the silhouette’s apparent position, captain identity, and the shift in camera height. This tests one actual continuity handoff instead of sampling unrelated scenes. If the handoff works, extend the pilot to the preceding and following shots.

For each pilot shot, agree on the intended edit length first. Write a motion prompt with a single camera action and explicit limits: what may move, what must stay fixed, where the motion ends, and how long the final composition holds. Review all three points of the clip—opening, middle, end—then watch the clip continuously. Put the two connected takes against the intended song passage and assess whether movement and cut timing support the phrase. Revise continuity references, camera amplitude, or durations before authorizing the larger batch.

Review boundaries and handoff

The completed batch has technical evidence for all 87 local videos: nonempty MP4 files, a valid container marker, 1920×1080 dimensions, decodable frames, and durations aligned to the requested three- or four-second settings. Five middle frames were sampled for visual spot checks. The results ledger records completed, authorized second attempts for 001, 016 and 075. The first attempts remain unresolved. These facts make the pipeline auditable and help a later editor locate the source material.

They do not amount to a full creative review, a complete visual continuity pass, or an audio synchronization check. Those require watching every shot and the assembled sequence with the actual audio master. The next owner should retain the treatment and its palette/continuity references, the stills, the manifest and editorial CSV, the result and download ledgers, and the local MP4s. Add timecodes and editorial decisions during the conform, then record any replaced shots with new versioned filenames rather than overwriting the original evidence.
