# BLACK STAR — Cinematic Music Video Shot Treatment

**Format:** Each shot is a separate 1920×1080 image (16:9), cinematic photoreal sci-fi horror. Never generate a multi-panel sheet or collage.  
**Visual palette:** deep black, cold steel, glacial cyan, sparse white highlights; restrained practical amber inside the ship.  
**Approach:** A story-led visual sequence. No band, instruments, performance, or literal lyric text on screen. Each shot is designed as a still-image setup that can later be animated in Kling. Keep motion controlled and readable; hold shots long enough to let the dread land.

## Continuity bible

- **Lead / captain:** Same man in every era: late 30s, lean, pale, short dark hair, close stubble, small diagonal cut through left eyebrow, tired deep-set eyes. His face becomes progressively more exhausted, but his age, bone structure, eyebrow scar, and eye color remain unchanged.
- **Ship look:** Compact, utilitarian exploration vessel; charcoal metal, worn edges, narrow cyan status lights, cramped corridors, broad forward observation window. No visible weapons or heroic military styling.
- **Suit look:** Battered charcoal-gray pressure suit with narrow cyan seam lights, scratched clear helmet, small rectangular chest lamp. Keep the exact suit and helmet consistent in all alien-world and exterior shots.
- **The presence:** Tall, unnaturally thin humanoid silhouette, matte-black surface with no visible face, no costume detail, no glowing eyes. It should read as a human-shaped absence. Never fully explain its anatomy.
- **Cosmic motif:** The black star is a vast black hole edged by a thin, turbulent icy-blue accretion ring; cracked dead planets and bending starlight recur. Avoid text and logos.
- **Crew:** Use a small, consistent crew of three or four adults in subdued practical clothing or matching work suits. When faceless, their heads are smooth shadow or lost in darkness, with no gore.
- **Image-to-video discipline:** One primary camera action per shot, subtle secondary environmental motion, no sudden morphs, no cuts within a generated clip, no new props/characters appearing mid-shot. Generate each shot separately and cut in the edit.

## Assembly notes

1. Generate each numbered shot as its own image/video clip. For strongest character continuity, reuse the same approved captain reference image for every shot in which his face or suit appears.
2. Keep most camera moves slow and simple; let the lyric edits, sound design, and light changes supply the acceleration.
3. For high-impact beats (shots 05, 11, 16, 26, 28), generate a mostly stable composition and add only the listed impact, door closure, or light extinction.
4. Use hard cuts for impacts and discoveries; use dissolves or match cuts for the transitions from space to home and from home to the cosmic reveal.
5. Keep the title treatment and any typography for a separate edit pass so generated footage remains clean and text-free.
