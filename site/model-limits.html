Production manual / Step 04

Read the model limits

Convert musical timing into a legal source duration, with handles for the edit.

Try the render-length calculator

Read the duration options before locking the shot list

Use the authenticated CLI's tool_list to confirm the generation tool, then read who_am_i for the current account. Under availableModels (or available_models), find the tool, its selected model, and the arguments entry named duration. Read its allowedValues, unit, default and any conditions that apply to the planned resolution, input roles, or single/multi-shot mode. Record the date, tool, model, allowed seconds and applicable conditions in the production record. Refresh the record after a model, tool, account or relevant mode change, or a parameter rejection. If the options are unavailable or ambiguous, continue planning edit beats and defer paid submission until the allowed lengths are confirmed.

The standard Kling 3.0 duration description says longer durations may require prefer_multi_shots. Check that condition when planning an unbroken take. Other generation tools have their own model entries. The multi-shot option may request intelligent shot splitting without exposing a separate duration/start-time field for every internal shot. Treat it as editorially approximate unless the live selected model schema explicitly declares per-shot timing inputs; references or a timing screenshot alone do not create exact cut controls. A render's minimum legal duration is a source-media constraint, not the minimum time that source must occupy on the final song timeline.

A 15-second length is a useful planning range, not a hard ceiling: longer takes are allowed when the story, continuity and live credit estimate justify them, up to the selected model's verified maximum. The split-and-new-still option is reserved for a creative change of angle/action: first listen for and mark a meaningful musical/story cue, retain the current still for 018A, create a continuity-matched new opening still for 018B, review the pair in the still animatic, then generate both clips. Never split at an arbitrary midpoint just to meet an obsolete cap.

Choose and validate each shot using this duration order.

  1. Decide how long the beat needs to play in the song edit from the action, emotion and musical cue. Do not lengthen a shot only because a model defaults to a longer render.
  2. Convert the desired edit range and useful head/tail handles to source frames and seconds at the chosen timeline rate.
  3. Read the selected model's live allowedValues, default and mode/quality restrictions. A default is a starting value, not a maximum; do not infer the maximum from the default or from a different mode.
  4. If one supported source duration covers the edit plus handles, choose the shortest such duration that still gives the action room to land. Estimate cost using that exact model, resolution, duration and number of submitted outputs. If the edit slot is shorter than the model minimum, render the minimum legal source and make a separate non-destructive trim for the song timeline.
  5. If the edit plus handles exceeds the selected model's maximum, first check whether another approved mode/model supports one continuous take. Do not split just to obey a planning target that was never an explicit creative limit. Split only when the supported duration cannot cover the needed take or when a change of angle/action improves the story; choose a clear audible/visual boundary, add a continuity-matched opening still for the next shot, and preserve the combined timeline duration. If the boundary is uncertain, listen and resolve it before still/video generation.

Record the chosen model, mode, render seconds, edit seconds, source in/out, handles and estimate in the manifest. Recheck the live model membership immediately before submission. If no supported duration fits the action, revise coverage/handles or choose another approved model; do not silently alter timing, slow footage, or split at an arbitrary midpoint.

For example, a 7 s edit plus two 1 s handles needs 9 s of source. On the verified 4.0 model, request 9 s. On the verified 2.6 model, request 10 s and keep the extra second for trimming. A 12 s uninterrupted edit cannot fit 2.6's longest source even with zero handles. Plan a cut and its matching stills, or choose an approved model and mode that supports the take.

Track edit duration (frames used on the song timeline) and render duration (the source clip including handles). Time the performance against the recording: setup → decision or action → consequence. Add head/tail handles where a trim, movement match, dissolve, or sustained reaction needs them. Choose the shortest supported render length that fits this performance and its handles. Use 15 seconds or less as a practical target for most generated clips; exceed 15 when the selected model supports it and the scene earns the longer take. Check cost, character continuity and action readability on long shots. A brief insert may use less than the model's minimum in the finished edit.

Creative timing range (choose a legal render length above) What can justify it Camera or performance decision
3–4 s Insert, visual punctuation, interruption, quick reaction Begin close to the action. Land one unambiguous change.
5–7 s A gesture followed by a readable response, short pursuit, one reveal Let the subject initiate the move. Settle long enough to catch the response.
8–11 s Tracking shot through a space, tension that builds before a decision, an exchange in two planes Use blocking or a foreground pass to change what the audience can see mid-take.
12–15 s and longer when supported A sustained performance or continuous action whose emotional effect depends on watching the change happen Design an opening position, a middle turn on a musical cue, and a distinct ending composition. Budget handles and inspect identity drift through the take.

If an edit exceeds the selected model's maximum after handles, redesign the coverage, overlap another shot, or split on a meaningful cue. If the selected model offers different increments, choose a supported duration and trim to the frame in the edit. A shorter supported model can cover a long beat with planned cuts. Agree on a different model if an unbroken long take is essential. Allocate the shot count after timing the story. Raw footage can exceed the song runtime.

Split overlong shots before generation only when needed. Compare the mapped edit plus handles against the live selected model's maximum and the project's current duration policy. If a split is required for those limits or improves the storytelling, locate the boundary on an audible lyric, musical event or clear action turn; do not divide at an arbitrary midpoint. Keep the existing approved still as the first part where it fits, create a new numbered opening still for the next part before video generation, and attach the same approved cast, wardrobe, location and prop references. Match screen direction, eyelines, lighting, emotional state and action phase across the pair. Update shot IDs, manifests, edit map and still animatic while preserving the total song timeline. Review the pair in sequence before rendering motion.

When the model minimum is longer than the song slot, edit the render down. Generate at the shortest legal duration that gives the action room to work, then choose the strongest source in/out frames to fit the approved song-timeline slot. Preserve the original downloaded Kling file unchanged and create a clearly named, separately versioned trimmed derivative or timeline edit. Keep the shot ID linked across both files and record source in/out, timeline in/out, frame rate, and the reason for the trim. Do not force every source clip to fill the timeline slot or slow it down automatically. If trimming removes the setup, decisive action, reaction, or continuity needed to understand the beat, mark the shot as a mismatch and revise its coverage, prompt, or edit duration rather than hiding the problem.

Put the decisive action inside the selected frames. A handle is spare footage the editor can trim. A nine-second render with one second of handle at each end gives a seven-second edit from source second 1 up to second 8. If the lyric accent occurs five seconds into this edit, aim the corresponding gesture at source second 6. Mark the actual frame where it lands after rendering and adjust the source in/out against the song. When a retime is needed, inspect hands, footfalls, lip sync and camera speed through the adjustment.
