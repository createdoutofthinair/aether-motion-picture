Production manual / Step 09

Produce the sequences

Move each shot from an approved reference to a traceable local file.

Choose pilot shots for musical and dramatic contrast

Select pilot shots from the most informative contrasting moments on the verified audio/event and interpreted tension maps. Where the song supports it, pair a high-energy or action-led passage with a sparse, quiet or lower-energy passage; choose a real planned shot for each, not a special test scene. Prefer a pair that also tests different performance or camera demands, and include a hard handoff if continuity is one of the production risks. The aim is to learn whether the stills and Kling direction can carry more than one emotional/cinematic register before generating the rest of the sequence.

Use Whisper or another speech recognizer only to help locate candidate vocal phrases and rough timestamps. It does not measure musical energy or determine emotional tension. Select the contrast using the audio-feature/event notes, the director's tension reading, and direct listening to the master. Verify each chosen passage against the song; if the recognition output misses or misplaces a lyric, follow the recording and keep the cue timing provisional until checked. Do not pick a loud section automatically as the highest-tension moment: a quiet pause can carry more anticipation than a louder passage.

  1. Estimate source seconds × current rate for each model and resolution. Include approved stills, alternate coverage and any pickup reserve. Keep source seconds separate from final runtime. If exact pricing is unavailable, record the uncertainty and the artist's approved limits.

  2. Start with the contrasting pilot shots selected above. These are real planned shots and, if accepted, remain in the final sequence. Cut the actual renders over the audio and inspect them together. Resolve repeated issues in the still, motion prompt or continuity plan before scaling to similar shots.

  3. Default to parallel submission for approved, independent shots. Once stills, prompts, settings and spend authority are ready, launch independent Kling jobs in a small controlled wave instead of waiting for unrelated jobs to finish. Before the wave, inspect live tool/model metadata and the CLI contract for batch size, duration, inputs and published rate/concurrency limits; record the date and evidence. The connected account checked 2026-11-20 published no numeric in-flight limit, so start conservatively and increase only while the service accepts work without throttling or errors. Keep a unique task ID and output path for every shot; query and review results independently. Keep sequence-dependent jobs serial until their prerequisite still, approved state or actual exit frame exists. Honor retry intervals, pause or reduce the wave on rate limits, and never resubmit an ambiguous request until its creation state is reconciled. A timeout never authorizes a duplicate charge.

  4. Keep sequence-dependent shots out of the parallel wave until their prerequisite still, approved state or actual exit frame is ready. Divide only independent shots into disjoint shot-ID ranges, give every worker the same frozen treatment, prompt version and approved reference packet, and assign each a unique output path. Maintain one shared ledger with shot/version, prompt, reference files and roles, settings, generation ID, returned work index, attempt, status, cost when supplied, and downloaded path. Record acceptance immediately and resume existing tasks by ID. Parallel workers speed up queue submission and review throughput; they do not make dependent story beats independent.

  5. Watch each moving take at normal speed, then scrub the cut points. Check identity, hands, physical contact, direction, camera motivation, focus, flicker, geometry, and whether the performer actually makes the intended choice. A valid MP4 header proves only the file container. A still or thumbnail cannot demonstrate motion quality.

  6. Cut each new take between its neighbors with the song. Ask “What changed? What made it happen? Where does my eye go next? Did I feel the lyric land?” Check action once across the cut, consistent eyelines, and adequate head/tail frames. Record accepted source in/out frames. If motion inspection is unavailable, mark the take awaiting visual review and supply a local preview for the editor.

  7. Diagnose misses before requesting a pickup. A late gesture may be fixed by slipping the edit. An unexplained location change needs a bridge. A face drifting during an orbit may need a shorter arc, stronger reference, or a different angle. Wrong prop hand usually means repairing the still pair. Change the smallest thing that fixes the story and check the pair again. Keep prior takes versioned.

  8. If a submission loses its task ID, preserve safe diagnostic information and check recoverable task history before spending again. If the first job cannot be identified, flag the possible duplicate charge and obtain the artist's decision. Refresh expired media links against the successful job. Download accepted assets promptly.

  9. Use a real visual acceptance gate before the full motion batch. A small contact sheet sampled at four points is only a screening aid; it can miss brief continuity breaks, failed gestures, or late unwanted motion. For the pilot, review every clip in real time at native resolution, compare it with its approved starting still and prompt, then inspect the action and both cut points frame by frame. The key prompted event must be clearly visible, character and prop identity must hold, and the camera move must remain physically and geographically coherent. Record pass, trim, or unresolved issue separately for technical integrity, story action, identity/continuity, camera, and artifacts. Do not infer a pass from a valid MP4, a good thumbnail, or a motion description. If a concern appears, review the full clip and its neighboring shots before making an editorial decision.

  10. Scale only after the pilot proves the prompt pattern. Choose pilot shots that exercise distinct risks (for example, a quiet hold, a character gesture, and a moving camera or prop interaction). If the key action is missed or continuity fails, revise the still, blocking, reference packet, or prompt and inspect the corrected pilot result before issuing the broad batch. Do not blindly regenerate an entire sequence. Treat every pickup as a separate costed job with a concrete defect, a specific corrective change, and a defined acceptance check; otherwise use an editorial trim or carry the documented limitation forward. Do not spend on corrective Kling jobs unless the current production's approved scope covers pickups.
