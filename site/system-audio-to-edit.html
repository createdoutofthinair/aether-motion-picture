Audio to edit: map cues into timecode

On this pageStart with a locked sourceImport sentence anchors from TrebloMake a cue sheetRun one beat/onset pass for each productionOptional additional local measurementsVerify at edit speed

Start with a locked source

Use the approved, final audio master as the timing reference. Record its filename, sample rate, channel layout, exact runtime, and the video edit frame rate. Do not build a cut map from a streaming preview or a temporary mix: even a small offset can move every later marker. Preserve the original file and make a working copy only when a tool needs conversion.

Import sentence anchors from Treblo

For vocal phrase boundaries, use sentence-level start/end timings from the exact matching Treblo song/version when available:

  1. Confirm Treblo title, artist, version and runtime against the final local master, then record the exact song URL and master filename/version.
  2. Copy each sentence's text and its displayed start/end as elapsed seconds from track start. Preserve order and repeated lines as separate rows. Do not infer boundaries from lyric order, derive word times, or use a player counter without checking the sentence edges.
  3. Enter the rows in Treblo_Sentence_Timings_Source.csv with section,text,start_seconds,end_seconds; keep the displayed decimal precision.
  4. Normalize at the edit's actual frame rate using the command below. The example is for 24 fps.
  5. Inspect row order, nonempty intervals and plausible durations. Check the first vocal entry, one middle sentence and the final vocal sentence against the local master. Resolve a version offset before using the timings.
  6. Transfer sentence IDs and intervals to the cue map as anchors. Decide cuts independently and review them at normal speed with the final master.

Do not infer times from page order or song duration. If Treblo shows untimed lyrics only, manually time just the missing sentence boundaries needed for planned edit cues by listening to the final local master, labeling those rows MANUAL_AUDIO_CHECK. If neither timestamps nor the local master are available, keep timing provisional.

Keep the untouched source rows as Treblo_Sentence_Timings_Source.csv (section,text,start_seconds,end_seconds, optionally timing_provenance). Default provenance is TREBLO_SENTENCE_TIMING; use MANUAL_AUDIO_CHECK only for boundaries timed by listening to the final local master because Treblo did not show timestamps. Normalize them to Treblo_Sentence_Timings.csv with the local helper:

python "Carry_The_Fire_Production\01_Audio_Lyrics\normalize_treblo_sentence_timings.py" `
  --input "Carry_The_Fire_Production\01_Audio_Lyrics\Treblo_Sentence_Timings_Source.csv" `
  --output "Carry_The_Fire_Production\01_Audio_Lyrics\Treblo_Sentence_Timings.csv" `
  --song-url "https://treblo.com/song/SONG_ID" --fps 24

The normalized file adds stable sentence IDs, source URL, timing provenance and frame-rate-specific timecodes. Check the first and last sentence against the final local master for a version offset. Copy sentence intervals into the cue/tension map as anchors; decide cuts separately. Treblo timing is the sentence map, while the final local master remains the sync authority.

When Treblo sentence coverage matches the final song, skip ASR, transcription setup, manual vocal retiming and ASR gap reports. Trigger a vocal fallback only for missing, mismatched or unusable Treblo intervals that affect the edit. Resolve only those intervals, keep the result separate with its provenance, and do not create word-by-word timings.

Listen once without stopping to understand the large musical shape. On a second pass, mark instrumental events that can support a cut or a change in image: downbeats, fills, sustained notes, transitions, and intentional pauses. Use Treblo sentence intervals for vocal phrase starts/ends. For each marker, write both a timecode and a short plain-language description. A marker is an editorial hypothesis; the sound and picture still need to be reviewed together.

Make a cue sheet

Use a consistent timecode convention. At 24 frames per second, 00:00:12:06 means 12 seconds and 6 frames, or 12.25 seconds. At 25 fps, the same frame field represents 12.24 seconds. State the project rate on the cue sheet and use the editor’s actual timecode display, especially for drop-frame formats. Keep the source audio’s absolute time separate from a shot’s local in/out duration.

A compact CSV can start with these fields:

Source,Marker,Timecode,Seconds,Event,Editorial use,Confidence
MASTER.wav,M01,00:00:00:00,0.000,Opening texture,Establish empty space,medium
MASTER.wav,M02,00:00:12:06,12.250,Illustrative phrase change,Consider a new visual beat,low
MASTER.wav,M03,00:00:24:00,24.000,Illustrative pause,Hold or cut on the next entrance,low

The times and descriptions above are placeholders to show the format only; they are not measurements from the HARROW recording. Replace them by listening to the actual master. Keep confidence or notes when the event is hard to place, and store the original source name with each export so markers remain traceable.

For each planned shot, add an edit in, edit out, action, camera direction, and source asset ID. A simple working record might include in_tc, out_tc, duration_frames, shot_id, action, and camera. Calculate duration in frames from the chosen edit points rather than rounding every shot independently to whole seconds. Leave room for handles when the shot needs to begin moving before the visual accent or settle after it.

Run one beat/onset pass for each production

Sentence timing and beat timing answer different questions. Treblo anchors the vocal sentences; a beat/onset pass maps the pulse, accents and instrumental landmarks. Run one pass on a temporary decode of the final local master for every production, even when Treblo timing is complete. Prefer a beat or signal-analysis tool already available in the project. Save the method and provisional grid. Run a second independent tracker only when the first grid is ambiguous or a planned rhythm-dependent cut needs more evidence; do not install extra packages for a clear first-pass map.

Optional additional local measurements

If FFmpeg is installed, silencedetect can flag candidate quiet passages in a local audio file without uploading it:

ffmpeg -i "MASTER.wav" -af "silencedetect=noise=-45dB:d=0.15" -f null NUL

Replace the placeholder path with the actual master path. The threshold and minimum duration are starting parameters, not universal definitions of silence. A quiet sustained instrument, room tone, or a soft vocal may be labeled silent; loud reverb tails may prevent a silence event. Inspect the reported timestamps against the waveform and listen before treating them as edit points.

Waveform peaks, RMS loudness, spectral energy, and onset detectors can help locate candidate events. They do not identify musical meaning. A transient may be a snare, a consonant, or a click; a slow swell may have no beat at all. Tempo estimation can halve or double the apparent beat, and changing tempo or syncopation will make a fixed grid misleading. Use these measurements to narrow the search, then decide by ear and in context.

Verify at edit speed

Put the cue sheet, actual picture, and final master in the editing timeline. Play each proposed handoff at normal speed from several seconds before the cut through the outgoing and incoming action. Check whether the movement starts in time, whether the cut lands on the intended musical event, and whether the incoming shot has enough time to read. Repeat at normal playback after any adjustment; frame stepping helps diagnose an edge but cannot tell whether the cut feels right.

For HARROW, play placements 20 and 21 across 1:59. The first pair of traveling steps crosses the stage. The next angle drops toward the floor. Check direction, weight and the moment of landing against the music.

Save the approved cue sheet beside the edit, record the project frame rate and master version, and update the sheet whenever a cut moves. That leaves the next editor with the reasoning and time references behind the sequence rather than a collection of untraceable guesses.
