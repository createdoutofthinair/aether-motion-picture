Shot 30 · Running into depth

The earlier take looked as though the dancers were running on ice. A locked camera lets the viewer compare their progress with fixed floor seams. The opening frame puts the runners close to camera with room to travel. This is the recorded pickup prompt.

Image 1 is the opening camera frame, setting, costumes and blocking. Image 2 locks the man, image 3 locks the woman. One uninterrupted take. Fixed wide camera, architecture and tile seams locked stationary in frame. From first frame they sprint away diagonally from near lower left toward distant upper right golden pillars, traveling the full width and depth of the stage. Each bare supporting foot sticks to one point on the floor as weight moves over it; powerful push-off from the toes, knees drive forward, visible airborne flight and opposite foot lands on the NEXT tile. Shadows track the bodies. They rapidly shrink in frame, pass the first gold pillar and keep running into the distance. Athletic real-time pace. No camera tracking, sideways gliding, treadmill motion or pose holds. The man is barefoot in a plain black long sleeve shirt and loose black trousers. The reflections reproduce the same bodies and clothes. Photographic natural skin, no extra people or titles. Silent.

Test the instruction against the render

Look first at a single grounded foot. Does it stay planted until the heel lifts? Then compare the distance between the dancers and the pillars at the start and the end. In the final edit this take runs for 139 frames, starting at source frame 37. Trim the lead-in until the first displayed stride already has weight.

Direct a smaller performance

For a three-second close-up, place the emotional change in the eyes and mouth. In the recognition reserve, he looks screen left, his jaw releases and a half-smile forms. Keeping the lens close makes that change legible against a quiet lyric.
