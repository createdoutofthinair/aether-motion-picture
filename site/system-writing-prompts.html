Constructing motion prompts from a fixed first frame

On this pageWorked repair: shot 016, “Then every light aboard went out”What the production record saysSubmitted promptProposed rewriteA versioned, single-variable testA style-neutral exampleKeeping a small crew continuous

An image-to-video prompt should describe a bounded change to an image the model already has. Start with the first frame as evidence, state what remains fixed, name one visible action, define the camera, and say where the shot ends. Put the next editorial cut outside the generated shot so it cannot be interpreted as an in-clip transition.

The worked example below uses the treatment, still, manifest, and result ledger for shot 016. The separate tabletop example is hypothetical and style-neutral. The examples distinguish source facts from proposed wording; neither rewrite is presented as a submitted generation.

Worked repair: shot 016, “Then every light aboard went out”

What the production record says

Item Recorded source fact
First frame 016_Corridor_Lights_Out.png, a 1920×1080 centered view down the ship corridor. Nested wall frames and ceiling strips converge on the vanishing point. The captain is a small, still figure there; cyan strips, a small panel at the far end, and their reflections are already in frame. Find 016 in the Black Star shot archive to inspect the opening still.
Story cue Shot 16 follows “Then every light aboard went out.” The treatment asks for corridor lights to extinguish toward the camera, leaving the captain at the far end.
Camera The treatment and manifest both call for a locked, centered composition and no shake.
Requested duration The manifest requests 3 seconds. The earlier treatment lists 4–5 seconds for this beat, so the submitted setup is a shorter edit unit.
Next cut Shot 17 is an exterior ship view where one hull impact sends ice dust drifting away. The treatment calls for a hard cut on impact. Keep that impact in shot 17.
Submission record The clip manifest records the submitted prompt below. The results ledger records the successful 016 attempt as completed at work index 0; the first attempt remained unknown. The ledger does not establish that the motion was visually reviewed.

Submitted prompt

This is the prompt stored for 016 in the clip manifest:

Animate the supplied still as the exact opening frame of one continuous cinematic science-fiction horror shot. Preserve the composition, character identity, clothing, visible figures, setting, lighting, and cold black/steel-blue/cyan palette. Corridor lights extinguish toward camera, leaving captain at the far end. Camera movement: Locked centered composition; lights go dark sequentially, no camera shake. Slow controlled motion with subtle natural parallax; only animate elements already present in the still. End on a stable composition. No cuts, no morphing, no new characters or objects, no text, no logos, no band or instruments.

The prompt contains the right story action and the right camera priority. Its reusable tail also asks for “subtle natural parallax” after specifying a locked composition. Parallax implies camera-relative movement, so it competes with the lock. “End on a stable composition” repeats the lock, while the general no-band/no-instruments clause is unrelated to this corridor motion. The same broad parallax and stability defaults recur in neighboring manifest rows, including 014, 015, and 017. Repeated defaults are not automatically helpful: keep clauses that protect an actual risk in this shot and remove clauses that contradict the camera or restate it.

Proposed rewrite

The still itself supplies the corridor, captain, light positions, color, and reflection geometry. Lock those facts before defining the light change:

Use 016_Corridor_Lights_Out.png as the exact first frame. Keep the centered, symmetrical corridor, its nested frames, reflective floor, far-end wall panel, and the captain’s small centered silhouette in the same positions and scale. Keep his existing helmet and suit silhouette unchanged; add no people. The camera is locked off: no pan, tilt, zoom, translation, shake, or parallax.

Hold the opening frame briefly. Over the next two seconds, switch off only the existing cyan ceiling strips one at a time, starting at the far end and moving toward the camera. Let each strip’s reflection fade from the floor with it. Keep the captain motionless. After the last strip goes dark, let the already visible far-end panel dim; hold the resulting near-black frame for the remainder. Do not raise exposure or create another light source.

End the clip in darkness. Hard cut after this hold to shot 017’s separate exterior impact. Do not show the hull, ice dust, or impact inside shot 016.

This rewrite keeps the first frame’s geometry and subject placement as explicit locks. It bounds the only action to the lights already present and gives that action an order and an endpoint. It also places the impact at the next cut, not inside the corridor clip.

A versioned, single-variable test

If review of a render shows camera drift, prepare a proposed 016-v2 by changing only the contradictory generic motion clause:

  • v1 clause: “Slow controlled motion with subtle natural parallax.”
  • v2 clause: “The camera remains locked off with zero parallax; only the existing light strips, their reflections, and the existing panel may change.”

Keep the still, the rest of the prompt, 3-second duration, model, aspect ratio, resolution, and output count fixed. Save v2 under a new version and compare the camera framing with v1. This is a test plan, not a claim that drift was observed or that a retry was submitted. If the actual review finds a lighting-order problem instead, change that one action instruction in a separate version; do not combine both edits and lose the cause of any improvement.

A style-neutral example

This is a hypothetical tabletop reference, not a Black Star production asset.

  • Vague draft: “Show a cup being used. Make it smooth and professional.”
  • First-frame locks: a white cup is centered on a saucer, its handle points right, a spoon lies on the saucer, and a hand already holding the spoon is visible at the right edge. Keep the cup, handle, saucer, counter edge, and background fixed.
  • Proposed rewrite: “Use tabletop-reference.png as the exact first frame. Keep the cup centered, the handle pointing right, and the saucer and counter unchanged. The visible hand moves the existing spoon in one small circle inside the cup, lifts it clear, and returns it to the same place on the saucer. Keep the camera locked. Hold the final arrangement for half a second. Add no ingredients, utensils, labels, or text.”

The rewrite names one action, limits it to objects present in the reference, and ends on a checkable arrangement. No genre or aesthetic direction is needed to make the instruction precise.

Keeping a small crew continuous

The treatment’s crew-count beat at 038 specifies three crew silhouettes seated apart, with the captain in the doorway. The next beats use crew visible through an airlock window (039) and silhouettes behind a frosted wall (040). For a prompt across these cuts, label the three crew by their starting positions—Crew A left, Crew B center, Crew C right—and keep that order and count stable. The captain remains a separate fourth person. When the view changes from seats to the small window and then the frosted panel, describe which of the same three silhouettes remains visible and which can move; do not ask the model to invent faces or new crew members. These position labels are continuity handles, not new character traits.

For every version, keep the submitted prompt, the proposed change, the source still, and the result status separately reviewable. A completed task is an operational status; it is not proof that framing, lighting, or character continuity worked as intended.
